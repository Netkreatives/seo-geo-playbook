# Principles

1. **Few hand-written pages beat many generated ones.** Sites with hundreds of templated pages lost almost all visibility in one spam update; the site that kept growing had fewer, tool-like pages and a named author. *(observed, see [recovery](recovery/spam-update-playbook.md))*

2. **No programmatic page matrices.** No city × service, no persona × feature, no locale-wide copy objects filled into one template. Each page is written for its own query, in its own language.

3. **Every page passes four tests before it is written.**
   - It is about something the product actually does.
   - It contains first-party evidence: your own screenshot, number, test or method.
   - The query is not already fully answered by an AI Overview or a vendor FAQ.
   - It has a named author and a date.

4. **Verify every claim.** Features come from the code or the live product, prices from the vendor's own page with a "checked on" date. If it cannot be checked, it is not published.

5. **Real images only.** Real screenshots and hand-built diagrams or tables. No generated illustrations standing in for the product.

6. **Write natively per locale.** Translation passes produce drift and errors. Small-language results are often easier to win than English ones: in our data the same page ranked 5–9 in Dutch, German and French where the English version sat at 50–63. *(observed)*

7. **Numbers come from tools, never from a model.** Search volume, rankings, clicks and citations are read from a data source. An invented volume is worse than a blank.

8. **Impressions are not visits, visits are not sales.** Report each separately. Under about 100 visits a month, report counts, not percentages. *(rule of thumb)*

9. **Do not turn a marketing problem into a build project.** "Build a free tool to earn links" is software development with an SEO label. Consider it only if you would build that tool anyway.

10. **Wait before judging.** A change needs two to three months of data; a spam-update recovery takes longer. *(rule of thumb)*
