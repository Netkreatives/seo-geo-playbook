# GEO: what is actually different

Most "GEO" advice is classic SEO under a new name. These points are the real differences.

## 1. Assistants search before they answer

One question becomes several hidden searches. Open the "searching" step in an assistant and read the queries. Each one is either a page you need or a site you need to be mentioned on. *(sourced)*

## 2. Being cited is not being recommended

In one of our runs (50 buyer prompts, four engines, one site): *(observed)*

| | Engine A | Engine B | Engine C | Engine D |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Product named in the answer | 0% | 6% | 18% | 22% |
| Our pages cited as a source | low | 54–78% across the other three | | |

Our comparison pages were used as sources while the answer recommended someone else. Track the two numbers separately.

## 3. The gap is usually off-site

In the same run our pages scored higher on a citability rubric than the pages that were actually cited. What we lacked were mentions on app stores, forums and third-party lists. *(observed)* A vendor dataset reports most citations going to third-party pages and only a few percent to the brand's own site. *(sourced; one vendor, 10 brands)*

So: your own pages need to be citable, **and** you need to be present where assistants look.

## 4. Answers move

Sources change from week to week. *(sourced)* One test proves nothing; run every prompt at least twice and compare runs over time.

## 5. Assistants get facts about you wrong

Keep a dated fact sheet (what it does, platforms, plans). Ask the engines, trace each wrong claim to the page it came from, and fix it there. Count it fixed when it stays gone across repeated checks.

## 6. Video

If the results for your main query show a row of videos, a video is part of the answer. Chapters from 00:00 and hand-corrected captions. *(sourced)*

## What does not need doing

- A score out of 100 for "AI readiness". The weights behind such scores were never validated against real citations. Read the findings, ignore the number.
- `llms.txt` is cheap to add and harmless, but there is no evidence yet that major engines rely on it. Do it last.
