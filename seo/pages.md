# Pages: what to write

## Write for decisions

The pages that get cited and that convert are the ones read just before choosing:

- X vs Y
- Alternatives to X
- Best X for one specific kind of person
- How to fix a specific problem the product solves

Pure fact queries ("how long does X last") are answered on the results page by an AI Overview, the vendor's FAQ, a forum thread and a video. A lower-ranked page there gets no clicks. *(observed)* Clicks come from fix, tool and compare intent.

## Before writing

1. **Search the query.** If most of the top 10 are one page type, that is the type to build. If they are all editorial posts and you sell a product, skip the query. *(rule of thumb: 6 of 10)*
2. **One page or two?** Two queries belong on one page when their top results largely overlap. *(rule of thumb: 4 or more shared results)*
3. **Name the one thing your page adds** that the top 10 do not have. If there is nothing, do not write it.

## On the page

- Answer in the first 40–80 words and name the product. AI engines lift passages, not pages. *(sourced)*
- Show first-party evidence and the method behind it.
- A "best of" list has 5–8 entries. Do not rank yourself first. *(rule of thumb)*
- One honest drawback per option, yours included.
- Competitor facts come from the competitor's own page, with a "checked on" date.
- Author and date visible.

## Keeping pages true

- When a feature, plan or limit changes in the product, find every page that states the old fact and correct it in the same release.
- Refresh a page that slipped by comparing it with what now ranks above it, not by adding words.

## Pace

On a new domain, publish 1–4 pages a week. *(rule of thumb; sources disagree on the number, not the direction)* Striking-distance work, title tests and pruning need Search Console data a new site does not have; leave them until month three.

## Withdrawing pages

Removing weak pages is a legitimate fix. Return `410` for pages that are gone for good, remove them from the sitemap and fix internal links. Do not put `noindex` on the pages that earn impressions while leaving the weak ones indexed; check per-page data first. *(observed: a hurried clean-up did exactly this.)*
